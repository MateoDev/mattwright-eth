The founding team — Sydney Lai, Shashank Sripada, and I — have resigned our operating roles at Gaia Labs (SayShell Ltd.). I have not sold a single token. I'm staying on as an advisor, and I believe in the open source project and network as much as I did the day we joined. Open source AI has much more to offer.

I'll be straight about the why, without airing things that don't need airing. For most of the last six months, this team worked — largely without compensation — to find a path forward for everyone with a stake in Gaia. We sat across from investors, vendors, and community members who needed updates, answers, refunds, and payments, and we did our best to deliver them, often while we were chasing the very same answers ourselves. We didn't have to do any of that. We did it because people trusted us, and that trust was worth honoring.

In the end, we couldn't land a structure that let the Labs operate at the capacity it needs. That decision now rests with the owners of Gaia Labs, and I'm leaving it with them — with respect, and with the network's interests still front of mind.

Here's the context, because the fact doesn't mean much without it.

When we started Gaia, my co-founders and I were focused on the building toward the future of work. We saw a lot of projects building world changing technology in internet-native governance of assets, automated payments in exchange for output, and digital identity, reputation built on timestamped credentials on a ledger.

All of this, fancy in theory, bureaucratic and messy in reality. We saw DAOs fall to their knees, we saw onlookers applaud to the demise of democracy as best as we knew it in capital systems. The problem was that none of this was built for us - we created the patterns, but this system was for the machine economy. Governance, automated payment and financial rails, identity, reputation systems. All the bells and whistles AI and AI agents would need to coordinate resources such as compute, data, intelligence — and in today's paradigm, context or applied context.

We just hadn't said it out loud yet.

## Where It Started

This began in the fall of 2023. By ETH Denver in February 2024 we'd decided to partner with the team that had been incubating Gaia, and we joined properly that May.

The first product was the Gaia node — an agent, and more than an agent. In 2024 it was an open-source container you could deploy on your own machine in under five minutes from the CLI: a packaged vector database, a RAG pipeline, a prompt manager, an API server so any permissioned party could talk to it, and an integration layer for any of the roughly 2 million open-source models on HuggingFace. The catch was that training the RAG by hand still took days.

The Gaia node was early, and almost nobody understood it at the time. This was pre-Eliza, pre-OpenClaw, pre-Hermes — the harnesses that make it obvious simply didn't exist yet. Now they do. With OpenClaw, Hermes, and XO (beta.xo.builders), you can one-click a Gaia node from a text message and train its knowledge base the same way, over text. The part that gets interesting is what you build on top of that runtime: private, permissioned, and external-facing environments where agents share context strictly on your terms.

If you want to help build that open-source future with us, my DMs are open.

## What We Built

It's worth pausing on what this team actually accomplished, because none of it was inevitable and all of it was earned.

Between spring 2024 and the end of 2025, we scaled one of the world's largest decentralized AI inference networks — 710,000+ active nodes across consumer, edge, and cloud, processing 3 trillion real inference requests. Not synthetic benchmarks, not testnet traffic. Real production usage. And we did it with a lean, globally distributed team on a net raise of roughly $20M, while our closest peers — Ritual, Bittensor, Gensyn — needed $50–100M+ to reach comparable milestones.

We built a billion-dollar brand and ecosystem on peanuts, relatively speaking. We raised Seed and Series A, shipped a token, and stood up a Foundation and a DAO. We launched Gaia Domains. We integrated 110+ partners across wallets, L2s, agent frameworks, and payments. We got covered in Forbes and Cointelegraph, ran the Token2049 Singapore afterparty with Diplo and the official Korea Blockchain Week afterparty with Chris Lake, and built a community of 437K social followers, 160K on Discord, 166K on Telegram, and 5M website users.

That foundation is the thing everything else stands on.

## What's Next for Gaia

The network is in steady hands. The ByteTrade team — the group that incubated Gaia's original kernel back in 2023 — is stewarding this next phase: leading the restructuring of Gaia Labs and the Foundation, and continuing to build Olares, their sovereign hardware platform. The network keeps running, and the people carrying it forward have the mandate to do it.

**On the Gaia AI Phone** — and I want to address this directly. We made the call to roll it back. Shipping hardware halfway is worse than not shipping it at all, and the people who pre-ordered deserve better than that. The Labs, under its current ownership, has taken on the device refunds and is working with the partners involved to make pre-order buyers whole.

What we're leaving behind is open: we're releasing Gaia Edge OSS — the on-device local inference and agentic-skills software we built — so it can run on millions of devices across many hardware partners instead of being locked to one SKU. The software belongs to the community now. That's what "open" actually means in practice. It costs us something, and it's still the right call.

## What the Future of AI Looks Like

Fast forward to 2026. AI is going parabolic — closer to some AGI paradigm, closer to a world where it's genuinely hard to tell whose data is whose. It's hard to keep pushing this manifesto when Claude is this good and keeps disrupting vertical after vertical — and when, frankly, people keep asking why you'd bother. But that has always been the tension, and it's the right one to sit in. Closed and open will coexist. The future is collaborative: massive frontier models with serious compute, open local models on edge devices, and agents working on behalf of other agents. The only real question is whether the open layer is worth building, maintaining, and defending. We still believe the answer is yes. More than ever.

Here's why it matters in practice. I've written before about the [Billion Agent Economy](https://mattwright.eth.limo/building-for-the-billion-agent-economy/), and about what it does to human work — really halves of the same argument. Agents need to run everywhere, hold context across sessions, pay for knowledge automatically, and coordinate without a human in the critical path. And the people with real context should earn from it directly.

Think about an e-commerce merchant. Their real edge is context no marketplace can see — live inventory, true margins, what actually sells together, which SKUs to push this week. As shopping agents become the buyers, that merchant runs a node that lets a buyer's agent check fit, availability, and price in real time, and earns on every query that converts — instead of renting that visibility back from a platform that owns the customer.

The same logic runs for a DeFi trader who'd rather sell access to a signal node that nets $1,000 than watch someone burn a hundred hours rebuilding it. A professor monetizing their knowledge as it moves across AI systems. A doctor running a private local model on their own hardware, sharing inference only when there's genuine demand. None of them should have to route every query through a frontier provider at three cents a call. They should run their own node, earn from their own knowledge, and participate in a network that rewards specialization over scale.

## Gaia v2: An Open Proposal

Gaia v2 is written, and it's on the table. I'm bringing it to the Foundation as an open proposal, in my advisory role on the Technical Security Council. The idea is the one the original node was always reaching for: turn the 710,000+ commodity inference nodes into agentic nodes — active memory, MCP discoverability, x402 earnings, verifiable on-chain identity — and stand up an internet of agent-run context sharing on top.

It's a proposal, not a plan of record. It stays pending until the team decides the next steps for the Gaia ecosystem, and that decision is theirs to make. The work is shovel-ready and the runtime to build it already exists. We're ready whenever they are.

## What's Next for Me

I'm full-time on XO (xo.builders) — the runtime where agents go to work. It's a separate company, with its own team and its own backers, and it's where my focus lives for the foreseeable future. The bet is simple: building an agent is easy now; running fleets of them in production is not. Agents are employees now — they onboard, they offboard, and almost no one has answered what data an agent gets access to or what it's allowed to leave with.

Organizations need permissioning, governance, and payment rails just to let swarms collaborate safely, and that infrastructure mostly doesn't exist yet. XO is built for it: open, composable, and designed so your team owns its context and can move its whole stack as the industry shifts under us. Shashank is advising, focused on verticalizing agentic orgs for enterprises and embedding XO where real companies operate.
