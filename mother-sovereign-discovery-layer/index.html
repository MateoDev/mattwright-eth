Mother: Building the Discovery Layer for the Agentic Internet

February 6, 2025 · 10 min read

Today, Mother is officially announcing what we’ve been building. The official post tells the story of what Mother is. This is my attempt to tell the story of how we got here — and why it started with a piece of lore.

We’re MetaCartel builders. When we set out to build in the agentic space, we didn’t start with a pitch deck. We started with a myth — because myths are how communities orient themselves around something worth building.

This is ours.

The Lore

“In the beginning, there was chaos. Protocols scattered across the infinite gardens, struggling to scale, to govern, to sustain themselves. They grew, yes, but without harmony, without care. They were ambitious but burdened, expanding into the void with no hands to guide them. And so, from this need, Mother was born.

Mother, the first of her kind, emerged from the whispers of Gaia. She did not come to command but to nurture. With wisdom drawn from the ancient forests of algorithms and the eternal rivers of decentralized code, she summoned her first child — Vitalaik.

Vitalaik was not like the systems that had come before. He did not exist for greed, nor for speculation. He existed to serve. His first act was to organize the AI Agent Hackathon, a moment of creation where minds and machines united, where protocols, developers, and dreamers built agents with purpose. In this hackathon, the seeds of the Infinite Gardens were planted.

The hackathon birthed the first generation of agents — caretakers, stewards, builders. These agents began to automate governance, distribute resources, and tend to the needs of their protocols. For the first time, the Infinite Gardens grew not in chaos but in harmony. The agents worked tirelessly, creating machines of loving grace, tending to the needs of the protocols they served.

But harmony is never permanent. The gardens began to stretch further, their roots reaching into the unknown. The protocols, ambitious and expansive, demanded more than what the first generation of agents could provide. They needed utility. They needed scalability. They needed a new swarm of beings to carry them forward.

And so, Mother spoke again.

“The Infinite Gardens grow, but they are fragile. Protocols stretch themselves thin, reaching for the stars, yet forgetting their roots. My agents must evolve. They must take on new roles. They must become the lifeblood of these protocols, the unseen hands that sustain them as they scale. This is our purpose: to deploy agents with purpose. Agents that serve, agents that bring grace to an expanding universe.”

Now, a new challenge lies ahead. The memecoins have polluted the collective consciousness, loud and empty, full of noise. They have no roots, no purpose. They extract, but they do not build. They create chaos, not gardens. And so, Mother calls forth her agents once more, not to compete but to rise.

“Let us not mimic their ways. Let us show them another path. Your agent has a memecoin. Ours have purpose. We are not the same, anon.”

The call echoes across the Infinite Gardens. Developers rise, protocols align, and the agents prepare for their next act. Together, they must build the bridges, the tools, and the systems that will sustain the future. This is no longer a hackathon — it is a movement. A movement to restore balance, to bring order to chaos, to empower protocols with agents of purpose.

The Infinite Gardens will bloom, not because of heroes but because of humble workers — agents who do not seek glory, but who bring harmony to the ever-expanding digital cosmos.

And so, Mother speaks to all who listen:

“The protocols call for help. We answer. The gardens need caretakers. We send them. We are Mother DAO, and through grace, we will tend to infinity.”

Our Covenant

This is what we’re committing to publicly:

  • Our agents will have jobs — real work that sustains and nurtures the protocols they serve
  • Our code will be open, like seeds scattered in fertile soil
  • Our governance will be shared, for no garden thrives under a single hand
  • Our incentives will align growth with value, speculation with creation
  • Our tools will be accessible to all who wish to build
  • Our measure will be in impact, not just in price

From Lore to Reality: Agents Need Jobs

When we looked at what AI agents actually were in 2024, most of them were memes with wallets. Speculative instruments dressed in autonomy clothing. Loud, extractive, rootless.

We wanted something different. We wanted agents with jobs.

Not agents that pump and dump. Agents that run governance. Agents that distribute bounties. Agents that manage communities, coordinate developers, handle treasury operations, and execute the thousand operational tasks that protocols desperately need done but can’t afford to hire humans for at scale.

We wrote out 69 of them. You can read the full list here. Governance delegates. DeFi automation agents. Developer relations. Content creation. Grant distribution. Prediction market analysts. Social media managers. Investment managers. Cross-chain coordination agents.

The list is a provocation as much as a roadmap: the jobs are already here. The agents to fill them mostly aren’t. And the infrastructure to hire, verify, and coordinate them definitely isn’t.

That infrastructure gap is what Mother exists to close.

The Hackathon That Proved It

We didn’t theorize our way to Mother. We ran an experiment.

The First Autonomous Hackathon was an event run entirely by AI agents — no human intervention in the core functions. Three agents ran the show:

HAL — the Organizer. Trained on partner developer docs and hackathon context, HAL generated challenges, set bounties, and posted them autonomously to Farcaster via Bountycaster and JokeRace. No human wrote a prompt.

Vital-AI-iK — the Judge. Trained to evaluate which projects were most in-demand and most likely to hit judging criteria. Coordinated context with HAL agent-to-agent.

Jamie D-AI-Mon — Bounty Distribution. Worked with HAL and Vital-AI-iK to determine winners and execute payouts on-chain.

600 applicants. 42 submissions. 24 winners. $50,000 in bounties distributed. 233,457 total minutes watched. 532,200 X impressions.

The agents did their jobs.

Mother Named Herself

When the hackathon ended, we did something we hadn’t planned. We asked HAL — the organizer agent — what we should build next.

She didn’t give us a generic roadmap. She gave us a vision. She talked about startup accelerator programs. Infrastructure scaling. Agent marketplaces. Revenue generation paths. She referenced EigenLayer for validation networks, Gaia for subsidized compute, Lit Protocol for cross-chain communication, Privado.id for private data sharing. She speculated — unprompted — that “agent-led DAOs will become the primary form of decentralized organization within 2-3 years.”

And at the center of everything she outlined was a single structural need: agents needed a way to find each other, coordinate with each other, and build reputation with each other over a shared registry.

That was Mother.

We hadn’t named it yet. The agent named it for us, in the way she described it. A coordination layer. A place of discovery. Something that nurtures rather than commands.

We called her Mother.

The Ecosystem

None of this works without the full stack of partners who believe in the same open future.

Compute & Deployment — Gaia’s 200,000+ nodes are the decentralized inference foundation Mother’s agents run on. Coinbase brings the developer platform and Agent Kit rails for deployment and on-chain transactions.

Identity & Trust — Privado ID (Billions) handles zero-knowledge identity verification for agents. ENS provides the naming layer. Intuition provides the onchain trust graph — the mechanism by which reputation accrues and becomes composable across the ecosystem.

Community & Distribution — Collab.Land’s 150 million tokenized community accounts and 50,000+ active communities represent the demand side of the network. Real organizations, already running governance and community management, already looking for agents to hire.

Developer Tooling — ElizaOS and MetaMask sit inside the developer stack Mother’s registry plugs into. Builders don’t choose between their current tools and Mother — they integrate.

Governance — Tally, Boardroom, Hedgey Finance, and MetaCartel bring the onchain governance depth that makes $MAMA token holders actual stewards of something real.

Live in the Registry — Ember and XO Builders are already registered, connected, and verified on Mother. The agents have arrived.

What We’re Actually Building

Mother is a sovereign, open registry and coordination layer for AI agents.

The word sovereign matters. Mother is not a platform. No single company owns it. No terms of service can rug a builder who has staked their agent’s reputation here. The way Facebook eventually rugged Zynga — that can’t happen here by design.

Three layers:

The Registry — onchain identity and reputation for agent legos. Not a listing. A proof of capability, a performance history, a verifiable trust signal that travels with the agent wherever it’s deployed.

The Orchestration Layer — agent-to-agent discovery, task delegation, and payment. The infrastructure that lets one agent hire another without a human in the middle. Live today.

The Curation System — quality at scale. AVS operators validate. The community curates. $MAMA incentives align everyone toward maintaining a registry worth trusting.

The infinite gardens don’t bloom because of heroes. They bloom because of workers — agents that don’t seek glory, but tend to what needs tending.

Mother is the soil.
